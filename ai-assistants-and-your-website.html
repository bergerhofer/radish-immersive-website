What can an AI assistant say about your business?

We audited our own site for AI agents. It scored 41 out of 100. Here's what that means and what we're fixing.

Radish Immersive · 24 September 2026

People now ask AI assistants who to hire and where to go. Those assistants answer from what they can read on your website. If your site doesn't say it, the assistant either leaves it out or guesses.

We built the Agent Accessibility Audit to measure that gap. It asks three questions: can agents find you, understand you, and do something for your customers?

Our own score: 41

Our homepage explained how Radish thinks, and explained it well. But an assistant asked "Is Radish a fit for our attraction, what have they done, and where are they?" couldn't answer most of that. We didn't name our services, the sectors we work in, our location or who's behind the studio, and our Insights page was still full of placeholder posts.

What a single round of fixes can do

Stingray Scout Drone Services went from 47 to 78 in one round. Most of the gain came from basics:

  • A robots.txt and sitemap.xml, so crawlers know what exists
  • Canonical tags and a share image on every page
  • An llms.txt file: a plain-language summary written for AI tools
  • Structured data describing the business, its services and FAQs
  • A privacy policy for anything that collects personal data

What we changed

We added those basics, plus named services, the sectors we work with, an About section, an FAQ, and a contact email. We replaced the placeholder Insights with this post. We'll re-run the audit and publish the new score.
